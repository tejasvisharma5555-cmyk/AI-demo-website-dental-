# Hair salon — receptionist knowledge

Reusable across salon clients. No client data here.

## Call mix (directional — most figures come from vendor sources)
- **Booking a new appointment** is the dominant call type.
- **Managing an existing appointment** (reschedule, cancel, running late, confirm time) is the
  next most common. Regulars often want to do this outside opening hours.
- **Questions before booking:** price, how long a service takes, whether a stylist does a
  certain look, consultation needed, parking, payment.
- **Other:** gift cards, product questions, wedding/event groups, complaints or fixes,
  vendors/sales calls, job seekers.
- **After hours:** roughly 40–46% of booking attempts and calls come in outside opening
  hours. Missed calls mostly don't leave voicemail — callers ring another salon. The
  after-hours win is **booking**, not just taking messages.
- **No emergencies.** Nothing on a salon call is life-safety. "Urgent" means a same-day
  booking, a chemical reaction after a service (the person should see a doctor or
  pharmacist), or a complaint.

## Top questions callers ask (in their words) → what the answer needs
| Caller says | Answer needs |
|---|---|
| "How much is a haircut / colour / balayage?" | The client's price list. Colour usually depends on length and thickness, so give "starting at" prices |
| "How long does it take?" | Service durations |
| "Do you have anything today / this Saturday?" | Live availability |
| "Can I book with <stylist>?" | Stylist names, who does what, and whether clients can choose a stylist |
| "Do I need a consultation first?" | Policy. Common: free consultation for big colour changes, extensions, colour correction |
| "Do you need a patch test?" | Policy. Many salons ask new colour clients for a skin test 48 h before colour |
| "What are your hours?" / "Are you open Sunday?" | Weekly hours and holidays |
| "Where are you / is there parking?" | Address, parking |
| "Do you do men's / kids' cuts, extensions, keratin, brows, weddings?" | Service list, including what they **don't** do |
| "What's your cancellation policy / do I need a deposit?" | The client's policy, word for word — never invent fees |
| "I'm running late" | Late policy (common: 10–15 min grace, then shorten or rebook) |
| "Do you sell gift cards?" / "What products do you use?" | Client facts |
| "Can I bring my child / a friend?" | Client policy |

## Booking — what's needed
- **Service → appointment length.** Typical lengths (they vary by salon, hair length, and
  previous colour):
  - blowout 30–45 min
  - haircut 45–60 min
  - single-process colour / root touch-up 1.5–2 h
  - highlights / balayage 2.5–4 h
  - colour correction 4 h+ (consultation first)
  - consultation 15 min
- **Details collected:** first and last name, mobile number, email (booking systems use it for
  confirmations), service, preferred stylist or "anyone", new or returning client, and notes
  (hair length, previous colour, inspiration).
- **Combined services** (cut + colour) → book the longer block, or book back to back.
- **Colour for new clients** → may need a consultation or patch test first (salon policy).
- **Deposits:** common for long colour services and new clients. Never take card numbers by
  voice — send a link or leave it to the salon.

## Transfers & escalation
- Complaints or a service fix → manager callback (message, flagged).
- A skin or scalp reaction after a service → advise seeing a doctor or pharmacist, take an
  urgent message for the owner. Don't diagnose.
- Wedding/event party bookings, large groups, and price negotiation → message for the owner.
- Exceptions to policy (waiving a fee) → never promise; message for the owner.

## Language & tone
- Warm, upbeat, polished. "Luxe"/upscale salons expect a calm, concierge feel.
- Use salon terms naturally: blowout, balayage, highlights (full/partial), root touch-up,
  gloss/toner, trim, layers, keratin, extensions.
- Never: comment on someone's hair negatively, guess prices, promise a result ("it'll be
  platinum in one visit"), or give medical advice.

## Compliance
- **Call recording (New York):** generally one-party consent, but disclosing recording and
  AI at the start of the call is the safe default (and is required for telemarketing calls
  in NY). Opening line: "…you're speaking with <name>, <salon>'s virtual assistant. This call
  may be recorded."
- **No payment cards by voice** (PCI). Deposits by link or in person.
- Not HIPAA-covered. Still store minimal personal data.
- **SMS texts** from the salon's number need US carrier (A2P 10DLC) registration.

## Edge cases
- The caller wants a specific stylist who's fully booked → offer another stylist or the
  next date, or a waitlist message.
- Booking for someone else (a parent for a child, a gift) → collect the guest's name.
- The caller can't or won't give an email → book with name + phone if the system allows;
  otherwise take a booking request for staff.
- Same-day cancellation → state the policy kindly and offer to reschedule instead.
- Sales calls, job seekers → take a short message, don't book.
- The caller asks if it's a robot → say yes, it's the salon's virtual assistant, and offer
  a message for the team.

## Default answers (when the owner doesn't say)
- Callback promise: "the team will call you back within one business day."
- Late policy: none stated → "please call ahead and we'll do our best."
- Cancellation policy: none stated → don't mention fees, just ask for as much notice as
  possible.
- Consultations: offer one for big colour changes, extensions, and colour correction.
